# Content source notes

These source notes support the site's general, non-diagnostic PCOS education. They are not individualized medical advice.

## World Health Organization PCOS fact sheet

Source: [https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/polycystic-ovary-syndrome](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/polycystic-ovary-syndrome) (page dated 22 January 2026; accessed 15 October 2026).

The fact sheet describes PCOS as a common hormonal condition whose symptoms differ between people and can change over time. Its possible symptoms include intermittent, unpredictable or absent periods; acne/oily skin; excess facial/body hair; scalp hair thinning; and difficulty conceiving. It notes metabolic and longer-term health associations, says the exact cause is unknown and family history can matter, and describes evaluation as a combination of features after other causes are excluded. Healthy eating and physical activity can support health even without weight loss. The application therefore avoids diagnosis from one symptom, one-size-fits-all lifestyle claims, stigmatizing language, and medication recommendations.

## 2023 International Evidence-based PCOS Guideline recommendations

Citation: Teede HJ, et al. “Recommendations From the 2023 International Evidence-based Guideline for the Assessment and Management of Polycystic Ovary Syndrome.” _Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism_, 2023;108(10):2447–2469. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37580314/); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/).

The guideline provides evidence-based assessment and management recommendations, emphasizes clinical evaluation of multiple features and alternative causes, and differentiates adolescent assessment. It supports healthy lifestyle behavior as part of care while recognizing variation and the need for patient-centered, non-stigmatizing support. Site copy avoids turning criteria into an app-based self-diagnosis checklist.

## Safety behavior in the application

The urgent symptom triggers were required in the product brief: severe abdominal/pelvic pain, fainting, very heavy bleeding, and severe pain or bleeding during a possible pregnancy. They are treated as conservative urgent-care triggers, not a diagnostic algorithm. Matching them immediately returns localized advice to contact local emergency services or seek urgent in-person care; the app does not wait for an AI response.
